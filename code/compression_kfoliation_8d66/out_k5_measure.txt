k5.2  DOES alpha RISE WITH k AT A FIXED POSET?  (P8 of PREDICTIONS.md)
==========================================================================================

  k3 proves alpha_S <= alpha_{S'} whenever S' refines S.  Whether it rises STRICTLY is a
  measurement, and it is the measurement pm-onethird's derivation actually predicts.

  alpha_finest > alpha_(k=2):  92 of 434 posets     (flat at 342)
    n=4 lt=[(0, 3), (1, 2), (1, 3)]:  alpha_2 = 0.387628  ->  alpha_finest = 0.414957
    n=4 lt=[(0, 3), (2, 1), (2, 3)]:  alpha_2 = 0.387628  ->  alpha_finest = 0.414957
    n=4 lt=[(1, 2), (3, 0), (3, 2)]:  alpha_2 = 0.387628  ->  alpha_finest = 0.414957
    n=4 lt=[(2, 1), (3, 0), (3, 1)]:  alpha_2 = 0.387628  ->  alpha_finest = 0.414957

  READ THIS THE RIGHT WAY ROUND.  92 of 434 is the effect pm-onethird predicts and it
  is real.  It is also bounded: every one of those climbs terminates at ((n-1)/2) gap_BK
  (k3.3) and therefore at or below 1 (k4).  ALPHA_k RISING AT A FIXED POSET AND THE CEILING
  RISING ARE TWO DIFFERENT STATEMENTS, and only the first is true.  That is E1 of
  PREDICTIONS.md, and it is the step where the derivation slips.

  PASS  measured: rises at 92/434, ceiling unmoved at 1
