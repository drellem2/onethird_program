S3b  ATTRIBUTION -- WHICH NOUN A LABEL-INTERNAL INTEGER BELONGS TO

  `lib03d1.embedded_counts` attaches the word immediately AFTER the
  integer.  That is right for prose --

      ...ROWS outside it, across 10 distinct basenames        14

  -- and wrong for a column table, which this arc writes as often --

      973ca61 ALL rows   ROWS  49  SITES  47  GAP  2

  -- where the word after `49` is `SITES`, the noun of the NEXT
  column's value.  Attaching it makes 49 a count of sites when the
  label says it is a count of rows.

S3d  WHAT THE REPAIR DOES NOT DO
==========================================================================

  * BEING IN THE POPULATION IS NOT BEING CLASSIFIED CORRECTLY. E6, filed
    before the code existed. `count_items` puts every integer in scope and
    attaches the noun its own words give it. Whether that noun is the
    right grain for that integer is a fact about the CODE that printed it,
    and the only way to get it is to re-derive the quantity -- which is
    possible only where a re-derivation sits beside the row. Nothing here
    re-derives one.

  * THE SHARED-GRAIN INTEGERS ARE COUNTED, NOT ATTRIBUTED. The 1481
    trailing integers sharing a line's grain are reported as SHARED and no
    attempt is made to give each its own noun from the column header above
    it. Doing that needs a header parser, this arc has none, and writing
    one to fix a row I found today is how the next instrument gets a rule
    nobody checked.

  * NO CALL SITE IS MIGRATED. `lib56dc`, `lib03d1`, `libbf79` and
    `lib70c7` are unmodified, no transcript of another tree is
    regenerated, and no number published by another tree moves. The
    repaired population runs HERE and is reported HERE.

  * THE TIE-BREAK IS NOT VALIDATED AGAINST GROUND TRUTH. `column_shape`
    agrees with a reading of five rows I did by hand. There is no labelled
    set to score it on, so its 233 resolutions are a rule's output and not
    a measured accuracy.
