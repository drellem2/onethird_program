S4d  THE THIRD VERDICT, AND WHY NO ARITY CARRIES IT
==========================================================================

  Both instruments above answer all 79800 pairs.  The arc has adjudicated
  7 of them.  `verdict` returns the third value at the rest:

  population: the unordered PAIRS of the corpus's 400 grain NOUNS
      ...PAIRS in the corpus vocabulary                         79800
          ^ one unit is one pair of grain nouns;  label's own noun: `pair`
      ...PAIRS this arc has ADJUDICATED                             7

      ...PAIRS answered UNADJUDICATED                           79793

      ...PAIRS `_classify` answers with a definite verdict      79800
          ^ one unit is one pair of grain nouns;  label's own noun: `verdict`
      ...PAIRS the open-set classifier answers definitely       79800
          ^ one unit is one pair of grain nouns;  label's own noun: `definitely`

      the arc has ruled on 0.0088% of its own grain pairs

  * THAT IS THE HONEST FORM OF `623` AND OF `370`. 623 is the number of
    pairs `_classify` calls the same grain. 7 is the number of pairs
    anyone has actually decided. The gap between them is not a defect of
    the classifier -- it is what the classifier is standing in for.

  * AND NO NUMBER OF SYMBOLS CARRIES IT. A function returning one symbol
    per word IS a partition; a partition IS an equivalence relation; and
    an equivalence relation has no room for `unknown` -- every pair is in
    a block or is not. Five values do not help. Four hundred do not help.
    This is why `add ROW_WORDS` is the wrong axis and why `make it five-
    valued` is the wrong axis in the same way: both stay inside the shape
    that cannot express the thing that is missing.

  * SO THE TICKET'S SECOND EXIT IS THE RIGHT ONE AND ITS FIRST IS NOT.
    `Report the 370 rather than silently returning NONE` is a change of
    SHAPE -- it makes what is unanswered visible. `Return a grain from an
    open set` is a change of ARITY, and S4b is what that buys: a function
    that splits `steps` from `iterations` at every one of the pairs anyone
    has ruled on.
