SECTION 4, RE-MEASURED OVER THE GROUPS WHERE THE TEST CAN FAIL.

The committed claim (target section 4, and its section 0 headline 3 and section 8
item 3):

    "Every extremal poset is rank 1 -- and tied with every other member of their
     group: rank 1 of 3 tied with 2 at n = 5, rank 1 of 4 tied with 3 at n = 6,
     rank 1 of 5 tied with 4 at n = 7."
    "Within a fixed e(P) the statistic does not distinguish the extremal posets
     from anything at all -- not weakly, but by an exact tie."

Two defects compound, and the second is the one that matters.

  (1) REPORTING.  The loop printed at most three rows per n and skipped e-groups
      of size < 3, so all nine committed rows are e = 3 groups.  Removed here:
      every e-group containing an extremal poset is reported, at every n, with no
      cap and no minimum size.

  (2) THE CONTROL GROUP IS VACUOUS BY CONSTRUCTION.  Call an e-group VACUOUS if
      every one of its members is delta-extremal: in such a group "tied with every
      other member" cannot fail, because there is no non-extremal poset in it to
      be distinguished from.  The e = 3 groups are vacuous for an elementary
      reason (Proposition V below), so the nine rows the document printed carry
      no information about whether qmass separates.

  NON-VACUOUS is the definition used throughout: an e-group containing at least
  one extremal AND at least one non-extremal poset, so that a tie is capable of
  failing.

  PROPOSITION V.  Every non-chain poset with e(P) = 3 has delta(P) = 1/3 exactly,
  hence is delta-extremal (the conjecture is tight, so 1/3 is the minimum).
  Proof: for an incomparable pair {x,y}, e(P + {x<y}) and e(P + {y<x}) are
  positive integers summing to e(P) = 3, hence {1,2}, so min(p, 1-p) = 1/3 for
  every incomparable pair; the max over pairs is 1/3.  QED  (Verified below.)

The re-measurement runs to n = 8, which the target did not reach.  A factorisation
of the multiplicities (levels.py) replaces the level-lattice inversion both prior
instruments use, which is what brings n = 8 -- 16,999 isomorphism classes -- into
range.

==============================================================================
4.  WHAT IS A HYPOTHESIS AND WHAT IS A TEST OF IT
==============================================================================
The n = 7 e = 9 group is where mg-a7b4 found this, and the n = 6 e = 9 group
was in the same table.  Those two are the GENERATING observations and their
p-values are not evidence -- they are the reason the hypothesis exists.

The n = 8 e = 9 group is a genuinely new population.  The target document
never reached n = 8 and neither did the audit.  At n = 8 there is exactly ONE
non-vacuous group containing an extremal poset, so the pre-specified family
of tests has SIZE 1 and there is no multiplicity to correct for.

  PRE-SPECIFIED REPLICATION, n = 8, e = 9: 6 of 20, perfect = YES,
  exact p = 1/38760 = 2.58e-05,  AUC = 1 = 1.000

Multiple-comparison position, stated three ways and all of them survive:
  * the pre-specified test is one test:                p = 1/38760
  * Bonferroni over all 3 NON-VACUOUS groups ever run: p <= 7.74e-05
  * Bonferroni over all 7 groups containing an extremal poset, vacuous
    ones included as if they had been testable:        p <= 0.000181
  * joint probability of all 3 groups separating perfectly, under the
    independent random-label null:                     1.29e-08
    (this one CONTAINS the generating observations and is reported for
     completeness, not as the test.)

READ THIS AS A DEFLATION, BECAUSE IT IS ONE.  In all three groups the set of
members whose BEST linear extension reaches qmass = 1 is exactly the extremal
set -- no non-extremal member reaches 1 on ANY linear extension.  So inside
these groups the separation does NOT need L*: 'max over L in L(P) of qmass(L)
= 1' gives the same split, and L* merely attains that maximum.  The separating
content is that the whole spectrum fits on SOME interval chain, not that it
fits on the MAJORITY one.

And L* is not the argmax in general, so that is not a theorem either:

n        posets   L* attains max qmass      the max is unique
5            16                     14                     13

(Posets with e(P) > 400 are skipped for cost; 2 of 671 at n = 7.)  L* attains
the maximum on most of the population but not all of it, so 'L* maximises
qmass' is FALSE as a general statement and is not claimed.

==============================================================================
7.  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
==============================================================================
* Not a counterexample statement.  The extremal posets satisfy delta = 1/3,
  which is the conjecture holding with EQUALITY.  Nothing here is evidence
  about any poset with delta < 1/3, and section 5.4 of the target already
  prices the proxy: frozen forces e(P) >= 4 while most extremal posets have
  e(P) = 3.  The separation lives at e = 9, so it is at least in the part of
  the proxy that Proposition 6 does not exclude -- and that is all.
* Not a filter.  qmass = 1 retains a large fraction of the population
  (section 6 above), so 'qmass = 1' is not a test for extremality; the
  separation is conditional on e(P), and computing qmass costs strictly more
  than computing delta (the target's section 6 measures the ratio).
* Not an explanation.  qmass = 1 holds iff every level with positive
  multiplicity is an interval partition of L* (levels.py proves the
  equivalence from m_X >= 0).  Why that should coincide with delta = 1/3
  inside an e-group is not established here, and the mechanism is open.
* Not about L*, inside these groups.  Section 5b: no non-extremal member of
  an e = 9 group reaches qmass = 1 on ANY linear extension, so the split is
  reproduced by max over L(P) and L* only attains the maximum.  The powered
  test of section 5 IS about L*, since it uses qmass(L*) as the variable.
* The effect DECAYS with n on the powered test (section 5), which is the
  direction every other trend in the target document also points.  Three
  groups is three groups.
