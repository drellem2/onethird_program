A4.2  AN ALL-n FAMILY ON THE BOUNDARY -- ordinal sums of tight3
==============================================================================
  P_k := tight3 (+) tight3 (+) ... (+) tight3, k blocks, n = 3k.  Within a block
  the relation is {3b < 3b+2}; every element of an earlier block is below every
  element of a later one.  delta = 1/3 and E[inv_e] = 2k/3 EXACTLY, by hand:
  an ordinal sum's linear-extension measure is the product over blocks, so the
  pair probabilities are the blocks' own and E[inv_e] adds.

   k |  n | delta   | E[inv_e] | eps_spec = 6E/(n^2-1) | closed form 4k/(9k^2-1) | primitive?
  ---+----+---------+----------+-----------------------+-------------------------+-----------
    1 |  3 |     1/3 |      2/3 |                   1/2 |                     1/2 OK | True
    2 |  6 |     1/3 |      4/3 |                  8/35 |                    8/35 OK | False
    3 |  9 |     1/3 |        2 |                  3/20 |                    3/20 OK | False
    4 | 12 |     1/3 |      8/3 |                16/143 |                  16/143 OK | False
    5 | 15 |     1/3 |     10/3 |                  5/56 |                    5/56 OK | False
    6 | 18 |     1/3 |        4 |                24/323 |                  24/323 OK | False

  DIRECTION, STATED BECAUSE IT IS THE WHOLE VALUE OF THIS TABLE: this family is a
  LOWER bound on the boundary maximum at every n = 3k, and it decays like
  4k/(9k^2-1) = Theta(1/n).  It is NOT an upper bound on anything -- the exhaustive
  upper bounds are A2.4's and stop at n = 7.

  AND IT IS IMPRIMITIVE FOR k >= 2, which is why A4.1 exists.  An ordinal sum has
  lambda_std = 1 exactly and is not a candidate minimal counterexample, so if this
  family were the only boundary witness the measurement would say nothing about
  the class that matters.  A4.1 reports the primitive maximum separately.

A4.3  NEGATIVE CONTROL -- does the sweep FIND a planted violation?
==============================================================================
  A sweep that reports 0 of something must be shown capable of reporting >0.
  Re-run A2.2's delta filter at a LOOSER threshold and require the counts to move.

   threshold | n=3 | n=4 | n=5 | n=6   (non-chain posets with delta <= threshold)
  -----------+-----+-----+-----+------
         1/4 |   0 |   0 |   0 |    0
         1/3 |   3 |   6 |   9 |   21
         2/5 |   3 |  11 |  78 |  219
         1/2 |   6 |  39 | 356 | 4823

  The counts MOVE with the threshold and they are 0 below 1/3 -- so the 0 in
  A2.2's 'delta < 1/3 STRICT' column is a measurement, not an inert predicate.
  At threshold 1/2 every non-chain poset qualifies, which is the sanity ceiling.

A4.4  THE NEAREST PRIMITIVE POPULATION -- since the primitive boundary is EMPTY
      above n = 3, where IS the primitive minimum of delta, and what is eps_spec
      there?  (A4.1 makes this the only realizable calibration that exists.)
==============================================================================
   n | primitive posets | min delta over them | # attaining | max eps_spec at min delta
  ---+------------------+---------------------+-------------+--------------------------
    3 |                4 |                 1/3 |           3 | 1/2
    4 |               27 |                 2/5 |           5 | no `e` exists
    5 |              275 |                4/11 |          11 | no `e` exists
    6 |             4070 |                5/14 |          14 | no `e` exists
    7 |            86278 |               14/39 |          39 | no `e` exists

  The `no e exists` cells are P15's guard firing on real data and are NOT a bug:
  at delta > 1/3 the >=2/3-majority tournament is INCOMPLETE, so the distinguished
  order is undefined and inv_e with it.  A calibration that quietly used the
  natural labelling there would print a number for a quantity that does not exist.
  THAT IS THE ANSWER TO 'why not calibrate eps_sup empirically': it is not only
  that the frozen class is empty -- it is that `e` itself stops existing before
  you reach any primitive poset, at every n measured.
