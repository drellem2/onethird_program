Populations, methods and null models are stated inline.  No claim here
depends on a random choice except the permutation p-values, which use
seed 20260730.  All spectra and all balance statistics are EXACT rationals.

Reminder of the definitions in use (see core.py and bridge.py docstrings):
  delta(P) = max over incomparable pairs {x,y} of min(p, 1-p), p = Pr[x<y]
             over the UNIFORM distribution on linear extensions.
  A COUNTEREXAMPLE is a non-chain with delta(P) < 1/3.  NONE IS KNOWN, and
  none exists at any size below.  Every statement about a counterexample is
  of the form 'IF one exists THEN ...'.  Where the worst-balanced posets are
  used they are a stated PROXY for one, and never called one.

==============================================================================
SECTION 1  THE BRIDGE OBJECT: majority tournament, L*, and concentration
==============================================================================
IF a counterexample P exists, then no incomparable pair of P is undecided,
so orienting every pair by its majority gives a TOTAL orientation -- a
tournament M(P) canonically determined by P.  If M(P) is acyclic it is a
distinguished linear extension L* of P.  Acyclicity is NOT assumed below; it
is tested.  Both branches are reported.

-- 1a  THEOREM: a counterexample's majority relation is a LINEAR ORDER -------
This is not tested, because it is proved.  Let P be a counterexample, so no
incomparable pair has p in [1/3, 2/3].  Orient x -> y iff p(x,y) > 2/3.

  TOTAL.  1/2 lies in the forbidden band, so no pair is tied and every pair is
  oriented.  Comparable pairs have p = 1 and orient with P.
  TRANSITIVE.  Suppose x -> y and y -> z.  Then
      Pr[x<y AND y<z]  >=  p(x,y) + p(y,z) - 1  >  2/3 + 2/3 - 1  =  1/3,
  and the event {x<y and y<z} is contained in {x<z}, so p(x,z) > 1/3.  The
  hypothesis forbids p(x,z) in [1/3, 2/3], hence p(x,z) > 2/3, hence x -> z.

So M(P) is a total, transitive, antisymmetric relation containing P: a linear
extension L* of P, unconditionally, GIVEN that P is a counterexample.  The
FORBIDDEN MIDDLE BAND is what closes the composition -- 'every pair has a
majority' alone would not, and Condorcet cycles are a real phenomenon in
majority tournaments in general.  That is the trap, and 1a' measures it.

STATEMENT DISCIPLINE.  The theorem is conditional on a counterexample
existing.  It says nothing about a general poset, and the sweep in 1a' is a
DIFFERENT population where the hypothesis is false -- its results are not
evidence for the theorem and must not be read as any.

-- 1a' GENERAL POSETS ARE A DIFFERENT QUESTION -- swept, and NOT claimed -----
M(P) here is the STRICT majority digraph of an arbitrary poset: x -> y iff
p(x,y) > 1/2.  Ties (p = 1/2 exactly) are left unoriented.  General posets do
NOT satisfy the counterexample hypothesis, so 1a does not apply to them and
nothing below is offered as support for it.  NO CLAIM of general transitivity
is made here: the sweep bounds the range in which no cycle occurs, and that
is all it does.

HONEST PRICING OF THE ADDENDUM'S QUANTITY.  R(P) < 1 is a true consequence of

slack: the condition is already met by real posets whose delta is far from

on its boundary rather than inside it.  So it is not a usable filter on the
search space, and the reason is structural (mean versus max), not a matter of
the range being too small.

==============================================================================
SECTION 2  THE FACE-SIDE BALANCE CONSTANT -- the one place a signal appears
==============================================================================
The action supplies its OWN balance constant, and it needs no linear
extensions to compute.  For an incomparable pair, over the weight that is
uniform on the P-compatible moves, let
    s   = Pr[the move leaves x,y in one block]         (pair not separated)
    q   = Pr[x's block strictly before y's],  q' = the other way.
The walk's own stationary marginal is pi(x<y) = q/(q+q') -- exact, proved in
SECTION 4, and checked against the stationary vector of the actual matrix in
selftest.py control C8.  Define, in exact parallel with delta,
    delta_walk(P) = max over incomparable pairs of min(pi, 1-pi).

delta uses the uniform measure on L(P); delta_walk uses a count of faces of
the order cone.  They are different functionals of P.  How close are they?

==============================================================================
SECTION 3  THE QUOTIENT SIDE: is Q(P) organised around L*'s own chain?
==============================================================================
L* singles out one chain inside the acyclic-partition lattice Q(P): the
partitions whose blocks are contiguous INTERVALS of L*.  There are exactly
2^(n-1) of them and every one is a level, because ordering those blocks along
L* is P-compatible (verified by the assertion in bridge.quotient_concentration).

Two statistics, both 1 for a chain:
  qfrac = 2^(n-1) / |Q(P)|                       share of the LEVELS
  qmass = (sum of m_X over those levels) / e(P)   share of the SPECTRUM

POPULATION: the TIE-FREE non-chain posets.  A counterexample is tie-free, and
on tie-free posets L* is UNIQUE, so both statistics are canonical.  On posets
with a tied pair L* depends on the tie-break and these numbers would not be
well defined -- those posets are excluded and counted.

n    tie-free  #extremal    qmass: extremal vs rest    qfrac: extremal vs rest
3           1          -                  (too few)                           
4           3          -                  (too few)                           
5          16          3  1.000 vs 0.825  z=+1.16  0.642 vs 0.446  z=+2.90
6          88          5  1.000 vs 0.734  z=+1.95  0.590 vs 0.316  z=+3.40
7         671          8  1.000 vs 0.593  z=+2.64  0.541 vs 0.203  z=+4.49

z is the EFFECT SIZE: (extremal mean - rest mean) / SD of the rest.  This is
one null model -- 'the rest' at the same n is the size-matched comparison
population, and it is the whole of it, not a sample.  IT IS NOT SUFFICIENT,
and 2a says why.

-- 3a  THE SATURATION CONTROL -- and it dissolves most of the effect above ---
Every extremal poset has qmass = 1 EXACTLY, i.e. its whole spectrum sits on
L*'s chain.  But qmass is bounded above by 1, so 'extremal posets have
qmass = 1' is only informative if reaching 1 is rare.  It is not:

n      tie-free     #qmass = 1     %qmass = 1   of those, %ext
5            16              6          37.5%            50.0%
6            88             11          12.5%            45.5%
7           671             20           3.0%            40.0%

So qmass = 1 is a large, mostly non-extremal club, and being in it is weak
evidence.  The honest one-sided statement is the last column: among the posets
that saturate, the extremal ones are a small minority.  A predicate satisfied
by all extremal posets AND by many others is a filter on the search space only
to the extent that it excludes the rest of the population -- quantified next.

SECTION 7  THE ISOPERIMETRIC QUESTION -- stated as a question, with the
==============================================================================
            argument actually available, and its gap named.

THE QUESTION.  IF a counterexample P exists, its uniform measure on L(P) has
E[inv(L,L*)] < |Inc(P)|/3, while being supported on all e(P) chambers of the
adjacent-transposition graph, whose degree is at most n-1.  Does 'most of the
mass in a small inversion-ball, but spread over many chambers of a
bounded-degree graph' force a contradiction?

THE ARGUMENT I ACTUALLY HAVE, AND IT IS NOT ENOUGH.  Unconditionally, only
Markov applies: Pr[inv >= t |Inc|/3] <= 1/t, so at least half the mass lies
within inv < 2|Inc|/3.  With |Inc| <= n(n-1)/2 that radius is up to n(n-1)/3,
which is 2/3 of the maximum possible inversion count -- not a small ball.  The
count of permutations with fewer than that many inversions is a constant
fraction of n!, so no counting contradiction follows.  Numbers on the actual
population, to show the radius really is that loose:

n    poset                             |Inc|/3     Markov r    Pr[inv < r]
3    0<2                                   2/3          4/3              1
3    (antichain)                             1            2            1/2
4    0<1 0<2 1<3                           2/3          4/3              1
4    (antichain)                             2            4            5/8
5    0<1 0<2 1<3 2<4 3<4                   2/3          4/3              1
5    (antichain)                          10/3         20/3         91/120
6    0<1 0<2 1<3 2<4 3<4 4<5               2/3          4/3              1
6    (antichain)                             5           10        551/720

WHAT WOULD BE NEEDED, AND WHERE IT ALREADY LIVES.  To shrink the radius from
Markov's 2|Inc|/3 to something like c|Inc| with c small, one needs a
concentration inequality for inv(L,L*) under the linear-extension measure --
the |Inc| pair-indicators are not independent, and their correlation structure
is exactly the FKG/XYZ same-side-covariance obstruction already recorded in
STATE.md as the (B-cov) half of the open (B) obligation.  So this question
does not open a new route: it REDUCES to an obligation the ledger already
carries as open.  It is written down here as a question with its gap named,
and it is NOT offered as a route.
