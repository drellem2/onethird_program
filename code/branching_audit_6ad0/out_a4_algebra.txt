A4b  section 5 item 5(a) and its USE in section 2 item 5.
==============================================================================

  The derivation, as af28 states it in section 1 row 2: an N-indexed
  family closed under the disjoint union the external product needs
  satisfies P_m + P_n = P_{m+n}, hence P_n = P_1^{+n}.  CORRECT: put
  m = 1 and induct.

  What section 1 row 2 then says: 'at P_1 = a point this is the
  ANTICHAIN sequence, i.e. the classical braid case'.  Correctly
  conditioned.
  What section 2 item 5 says: 'the N-grading it presupposes forces
  disjoint powers, WHICH LANDS BACK AT THE CLASSICAL ANTICHAIN CASE'.
  The condition has been dropped, and the conclusion does not follow:
  P_1 is an arbitrary finite poset.

  Exhibiting the families the dropped condition loses.  For each
  choice of P_1, P_n = P_1^{+n} is a legitimate N-graded +-closed
  family, and it is the antichain sequence only for P_1 = a point.

   P_1                 |P_2|  |F(P_2)|  |AC(P_2)|  P_2 an antichain?
   a point                  2         3          2  yes
   the 2-chain              4        26         14  NO
   the 2-antichain          4        75         15  yes
   the 3-chain              6       252        106  NO
   V (one below two)        6       730        174  NO

  So the derivation constrains the family to disjoint powers but does
  NOT constrain it to antichains.  section 2 item 5's reason is wrong;
  its conclusion ('no tower') still stands, but on the OTHER leg --
  the unitality failure of A3c, which applies to every P_1 equally.
