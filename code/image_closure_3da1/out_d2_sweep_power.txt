d2.2  and it could not have been otherwise — the sweep tests nothing beyond c2.1
==============================================================================
  [GREEN] every one of the 300 maxima over R_n is attained at a vertex of M_n
       So `0 separations` is a RESTATEMENT of vert(M_n) subset R_n, not independent evidence for
       it.  A linear functional is maximised over a polytope at a vertex; every vertex is in the
       image; therefore no direction can separate, at any sample size.  The sweep has no power
       against its own hypothesis -- which is what `vacuity guard` should be read to mean.

==============================================================================
   Plant: the image with ONE vertex removed.  Its hull is strictly smaller than M_n, so a
   separation EXISTS.  The question is how often 300 random directions notice.

   n | n! | directions with a UNIQUE argmax | detects delta_id removed | pooled rate | 1/n!
  ---+----+---------------------------------+--------------------------+-------------+-------
   3 |  6 |                             265 |                       56 |      0.1472 | 0.1667
   4 | 24 |                             256 |                       14 |      0.0356 | 0.0417
   5 | 120 |                             232 |                        2 |      0.0064 | 0.0083
  [GREEN] a single removed vertex is invisible to the overwhelming majority of directions
       The pooled rate is the chance that a uniformly chosen one of the n! single-vertex
       removals is caught by a uniformly chosen direction, and it tracks 1/n! -- a direction
       catches a removal only when it is maximised UNIQUELY at the removed point.
       CONSEQUENCE FOR THE ESTATE, and it is the reason this arm is not a footnote: a direction
       sweep returning 0 separations is near-zero evidence that a subset of M_n is convexly
       large.  Where a claim of that shape is load-bearing it needs the containment argument,
       which is what mg-c776 has and what this directory re-proves at d1.
