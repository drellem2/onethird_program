    ⚠️  READ THE 21 BOUNDARY FAILURES CORRECTLY -- THEY ARE AN ARTEFACT OF THE PROXY,
    NOT A REFUTATION OF DANIEL'S CLAIM.  On the boundary class the most-balanced pair
    sits at EXACTLY p = 2/3, so at N = 3 it has g = 2 and no good run of 3 exists in
    ANY ordering.  Under the STRICT hypothesis p_xy > 2/3 that cannot happen:
      g > 2N/3 with g an integer forces g >= 3 as soon as N >= 3, and a poset with an
      incomparable pair and N = 2 has that pair at exactly 1/2, so is not frozen.
    SO THE NECESSARY CONDITION FOR Q1 IS A THEOREM UNDER THE STRICT HYPOTHESIS, and the
    boundary class -- which is the right proxy for the a-arm statements -- is the WRONG
    proxy for this one, because this claim is sensitive to the strictness that the
    boundary gives up.  That is a scope fact about the instrument and it is stated here
    rather than discovered by whoever re-reads the 21.

    AND THE SUFFICIENT SIDE HAS A HAND PROOF TOO, quantified: assign triples one edge
    at a time; at most 3(m-1) extensions are already used, so a system exists whenever
      min_xy g_xy > 3(m-1),  which under frozen holds whenever  2N/3 >= 3m - 2.
    The `greedy` column counts where that certificate fires.  Between the two
    certificates Q1 is DECIDED-TRUE at 40 posets; the 67 undecided ones have neither
    certificate and are NOT counterexamples -- disjointness is sufficient, not
    necessary, and overlapping runs are allowed by the statement.

m3  Q3 (the GRAY-CODE restriction) -- and why the run condition is nearly free there
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    THE STRUCTURAL FACT, measured over 1378 runs of three consecutive path vertices:
