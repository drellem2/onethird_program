T1e  AC(P) is a subset of Pi[n] for EVERY poset P -- degree is respected
==============================================================================

  The claim under test is only that the poset side lives in the same
  degree.  The stronger guess 'AC(P) = Pi[n] iff P is an antichain' was
  tested here and is FALSE; the counts below are what refuted it and
  they are printed rather than dropped.

   n  labelled posets  AC(P) subset of Pi[n]  AC(P) = Pi[n]  antichains
   1                1                      1              1           1
   2                3                      3              3           1
   3               19                     19             13           1
   4              219                    219             45           1

  Every one of the 242 labelled posets to n = 4 has AC(P) inside Pi[n].
  REFUTED HYPOTHESIS, kept on the record: AC(P) = Pi[n] holds for 3 of 3
  posets at n = 2, 13 of 19 at n = 3 and 45 of 219 at n = 4, against 1
  antichain at each n.

  CORRECTED 2026-07-30 (mg-6f61), on mg-a61f's X1.  This block used to
  ASSERT 'the smallest poset with AC(P) != Pi[n] is {a<c, b<d}'.  That
  is FALSE, and the table printed eleven lines above refutes it: 19 - 13
  = 6 labelled posets at n = 3 already fail.  The claim is now COMPUTED
  rather than asserted, from the same sweep as the table.

  smallest n with a witness : 3
  labelled witnesses there  : 6
  one of them               : 0<1, 0<2, 1<2

  The smallest is the 3-ELEMENT CHAIN a < b < c, where the partition
  {a,c}|{b} has a 2-cycle in the quotient: a < b sends {a,c} to {b} and
  b < c sends {b} back to {a,c}.  Six labelled posets at n = 3, one
  isomorphism class.  {a<c, b<d} IS a witness and is NOT the smallest;
  the reason originally given -- two blocks B, C with b1 < c1 and
  c2 < b2 -- forces |B| >= 2 and |C| >= 1, hence n >= 3, and n = 3 is
  attained.  See code/species_repair_6f61/out_r1_smallest.txt.
