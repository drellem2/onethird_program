## Appendix A — Audit-stage process (durable home for mg-3a3a)

**Standing process (Daniel directive 2026-07-19).** Every onethird research deliverable carrying a mathematical / `[PROVEN]` claim gets an **INDEPENDENT audit polecat** (dispatched by mayor, fresh context, never the authoring polecat) on `origin/main` **after the refinery merge and before pm-onethird's PM review**. The verdict routes to pm-onethird, who reviews it critically (second-line, not sole verifier) and owns STATE.md + the Daniel report. Merge stays a code-gate (build/test); this is a separate research-gate on the review step. `BROKEN` finding ⇒ annotate STATE.md (canonical) + a doc-pointer. Trigger: math/`[PROVEN]` claims only, NOT trivial doc-fixes. This is the durable home mayor's dispatch memory points at.

**Pre-file the audit ticket with the research ticket (added 2026-07-29, from the mg-88bd miss).** **The audit ticket is pre-filed alongside the research ticket, depending on it, because the audit must not depend on anyone noticing the merge.** The stage has no trigger — dispatch happens on the coordinator's polling cycle, and the PM watching a single product will often see a merge first. A pre-filed audit exists before the deliverable does, so its existence is structural rather than contingent on observation. This is *why* the pattern exists, not a caveat about it: on 2026-07-29 mg-88bd merged ~13 minutes after filing, inside the window between mayor's end-of-cycle check and its next wakeup, and reached pm-onethird unaudited carrying 589 lines of `[PROVEN]` claims and a refutation of canonical work. The stage's "audit is first-line, PM is second" ordering was never a guarantee — it was a race mayor usually won by accident. The fix is to remove the race, not to shorten it. Operationally:

- Pre-file for **any** research ticket expected to produce a `[PROVEN]` deliverable. This is the default, not a judgement call.
- **A RED deliverable is not a reason to skip it.** mg-a58f came back RED for its own brief and its audit was the most valuable thing that happened to it.
- If the parent arc is abandoned outright, **shelve** the pre-filed audit. That is a cheap wrong outcome next to an unaudited refutation of canonical work reaching a PM.

**Reusable audit-polecat brief (mayor fills `<path>` + prior-doc list):**

> You are an INDEPENDENT adversarial auditor of a onethird research deliverable at `<path>`. You did NOT author it. Try to BREAK its claims; do not be charitable. Read it (and any prior docs it builds on or refutes). Return a structured verdict.
>
> **1. CLAIM LEDGER (mandatory, exhaustive).** Enumerate EVERY claim tagged `[PROVEN]` — boxed theorems AND in-prose reductions / lemmas / "proven as a reduction" steps. Scan the whole document; do NOT rely on a curated short list (that pre-filter is where errors hide). For each: CONFIRMED / PLAUSIBLE / BROKEN.
> **2. RE-DERIVE, don't trust the label.** For each `[PROVEN]` claim: reconstruct the proof independently; check the DIRECTION of every inequality (lower-vs-upper conflation is the classic error — e.g. "`Var ≥ A` and `A ≤ B` ⟹ `Var ≤ B`" is INVALID); independently RECOMPUTE any small (n≤4) hand-check rather than reading it.
> **3. LABEL AUDIT.** Confirm every `[HEURISTIC]`/`[BLOCKED]`/conjectural claim is labeled correctly and NOT upsold in the §0/summary. Flag any heuristic promoted to a proven-sounding headline.
> **4. SCOPE CHECK.** Does the §0/headline match what is actually proven? Catch (a) strawman refutations — refuting a statement STRONGER than the real conjecture; state exactly which statement is refuted and whether it IS the real one; (b) "X is FALSE" / "reopens" / "converges" headlines that over-read the body.
> **4b. STRENGTH CHECK.** If the deliverable proposes a hypothesis, sub-lemma, or "first lemma" as a TARGET, derive what that hypothesis IMPLIES before assessing it — run it **forward**. Verify it is weaker than the theorem it is a step toward. **A sufficient condition that implies the goal is not progress toward the goal.** And check the **falsifier's quantifier** — a falsifier valid at one distinguished element is not valid at the max.
> **4c. PROPOSED-`STATE.md`-TEXT AUDIT.** **The text a deliverable proposes for `STATE.md` is a primary audit target in its own right, not a summary of the audited thing.** Audit it against the body and the ledger, clause by clause, and check every quantifier that appears in it. **Three deliverables now** have had sound mathematics and an overstated proposed row, and the row is the artifact that outlives the deliverable. **And on the fourth (mg-09ea's target) it fired three times inside a single document** — so do not stop at the first instance: **diff every summary artifact against the body clause by clause.** The §0 points, the ledger rows and the proposed `STATE.md` row are three separate summaries of the same body, and they fail independently.
> **4d. GENERALISATION AUDIT.** **Find whichever statement in the deliverable is the most general one it wrote, and audit *that* first — wherever it sits.** Not the headline; the **generalisation step**. **⭐ TARGET ZERO, AND IT COMES BEFORE THE GENERALISATION STEP: ENUMERATE WHAT THE SUBJECT ADDED BEYOND WHAT IT WAS ASKED FOR, AND AUDIT THAT SET FIRST. This holds for ANY subject, not only a landing, and it holds for a landing checking its own work — see *"A LANDING THAT ADDS A ROW BEYOND ITS BRIEF"* below, upgraded 2026-07-30 from an observation to this first-look instruction. That is where the ninth firing sat, it is the first BROKEN mathematics in this arc, and it is where the worst finding has sat in EVERY generation since the rule landed (mg-3c24, mg-6653, mg-e720).** **NINE firings AT LEAST, at a new location every time, and they do NOT share one tally** (recounted 2026-07-30 by mg-1319 from mg-5630 §5.2 — the previous *"five for five"* here was stale, contradicted the table below, and its single running count is exactly what let one commit report three different numbers; eighth added 2026-07-30 from mg-86a3 by mg-a806; ninth added 2026-07-30 from mg-d39d by mg-a2bd, and **the number is a FLOOR — merged-but-unlanded audits are not counted**; see *"STEP 4d HAS NOW FIRED AT NINE LOCATIONS"* below for the split, the boundary and the floor): mg-d112, mg-e35c and mg-f825 each carried a broken universal *in the headline*; mg-c8c6's target had a **CORRECT headline** and a broken universal in the **new theorem it introduced to explain the headline**; mg-09ea's target had a correct headline, a correct ledger row, and a broken universal in the **prose of the section it introduced to replace the refuted picture**; mg-e0ce's target asserted a universal in `n` off `n ≤ 5` witnesses **with no proof in the document** — the one firing whose statement was **true**, repaired by an upgrade; mg-5630's target had **0 BROKEN mathematics** and an over-wide claim about **its own control coverage**, i.e. about method rather than about posets. **mg-86a3's target had 0 BROKEN mathematics, a headline carried by a theorem that survived independent rebuild, and a NUMERIC SCOPE THRESHOLD inside a negative result — `"undecided exactly where |L(P)| <= 4"`, read off the population it was verified on and false one `n` further out, protected by the hedge *"on the tested population"*. New axis to add to your list: audit the SCOPE CLAUSES ATTACHED TO NEGATIVES. They read as modest, which is why nobody presses them, and a hedge that makes a false universal survive is doing all the work.** **The pattern is not "these documents over-claim their headlines". It is "these documents over-claim whichever statement is the most general one they wrote that day." The generalisation step is the object to audit first, wherever it sits.** So: ***"stop auditing these documents' headlines first."*** **Run 4d yourself even when the deliverable ran it on itself** — mg-09ea's target did, carefully, and still missed (see below). And **check every axis of scope, not only `n`**: scope in *regime* and the *inference* itself are separate axes and were the two the self-audit did not press.
> **Method — the locked-parameter test, unchanged, and still the test that catches it.** For every universal or non-existence claim (*"no `X` exists"*, *"every"*, *"the only route"*, *"nothing"*, *"provably"*), identify the family or construction that establishes it and ask **what that family holds fixed**. A witness family with a locked relation between its parameters constrains only its own sequence, and cannot establish a claim quantified over the parameter it locked.
> **Mechanism to name explicitly (mg-c8c6's addition): a property incidental to the instance, read as a law.** In `W`/`W*` the certificates happen to be **local** — only `x`'s own pairs change, because that family's `A`-side has *two* maximal elements — and that incidental locality became a claimed law about **all** certificates, in a single subordinate clause, with no hypothesis and no remark. It is the same shape as `W` locking `ε = 1/n`. So at every promotion from instance to theorem, ask: **which property of the instance is doing the work, and was it ever hypothesised?** The arithmetic has not been wrong once in this arc; the generalisation has been wrong **five times out of five**.
> **5. OBJECT / COORDINATE CHECK.** Distinguish static functionals of the stationary measure (e.g. `λ_std`) from dynamical generator gaps (e.g. `λ₂^BK`); state which object/chain each claim concerns; flag any conflation.
> **6. CROSS-DOC CONSISTENCY.** Does the deliverable refute / re-diagnose / supersede any claim in an already-merged doc? If so, name the exact prior claim, its label there, and whether the refutation is sound. (Prior blessed work can be wrong — check.)
> **7. CONSTRAINT COMPLIANCE.** Verify the deliverable honored the ticket's constraints (no enumerations / datasets / scripts beyond allowance — hard numeric stop). Flag any committed dataset/script/large enumeration.
> **8. VERDICT.** Return: the per-claim ledger; an overall CONFIRMED / PLAUSIBLE / OVERSTATED / BROKEN for "the claims are sound and correctly labeled"; any state-changes / refutations of prior work; and the honest NET (real progress vs relocation/re-description vs vacuous). Be concrete about every BROKEN/PLAUSIBLE step.
>
> Independence + adversarial posture are the point: assume the FLASHIEST claim is the most likely to be wrong, and scrutinize hardest exactly where the deliverable changes program state. This IS the deliverable — raw, structured, no preamble.

**How a pre-filed audit names a target it cannot yet see (added 2026-07-29, lands with the pre-file rule above).** **A pre-filed audit ticket cannot name its target.** At filing time the deliverable does not exist — no path, no commit, possibly no filename — so the template's `<path>` slot is left blank. The hazard is an auditor who resolves that blank loosely and ends up **auditing the parent ticket's description of the work rather than the artifact the work produced.** Those are different documents and only one of them is evidence. So a pre-filed body must carry, in place of `<path>`:

> **Target (to be resolved by you, the auditor).** The artifact under audit is the deliverable merged by parent work item `<parent-id>` in repo `<absolute repo path>`. **Derive its path from that parent's merge commit** — find the merge, read the files it added or changed, and audit the merged artifact at that path. Do not audit the parent ticket's body: **the ticket description is context, not the object under audit** — it states what was intended, the merged artifact is what was done, and the audit is of what was done. If the merge commit touches more than one candidate document, say which you chose and why; if you cannot find the merge, stop and report that rather than guessing a target.

That gives "which document am I auditing" one answer with a provenance instead of a guess.

**Dispatch-time targeting is still the coordinator's, not the template's.** The pre-file fixes *existence* and the derive-from-merge-commit rule fixes *identity*; neither supplies **targeting** — which claims to press hardest, and in what order. The coordinator fills that in at dispatch, once the deliverable is readable. That is where the value has landed both times so far: pressing Theorem 3.3 first on mg-d112 was targeting, not template.

**Does a REPAIR need a fresh audit? The narrowing test (added 2026-07-21, from mg-8ff1).** A repair

when the correction originates in the audit's own finding, a "fresh" audit would largely be
auditing the auditor's output, which is not independent. Worked example: mg-8ff1's F1 repair

Retro-linked on 2026-08-12: `mg-2f44 → mg-07fd`, `mg-8d63 → mg-5cba`, `mg-b417 → mg-5cba`.
- **An audit that UPHOLDS its target carries `audit-clean`.** It has no repair ticket and correctly never will, so it can never be answered by a successor; without a marker it is a **permanent** false report. `mg-a0d6` is the store's only instance and carries it. **An audit that passes is a RESULT, not an omission** — and *coverage* and *correctness* are separate defects here, which is why closing one never closed the other.

**THE SECOND TAG HAD TWO NAMES FOR NINETY MINUTES AND NOW HAS ONE: `audit-clean` (consolidated 2026-08-12 by mg-9134, on pm-onethird's decision).** mg-a882 introduced `audit-verdict-pass` without knowing `audit-clean` already existed — armed by mg-7ff8, printed in `pogo doctor --check`'s own remedy text, and published in pogo's `docs/CONFIGURATION.md`. mg-a518 found the collision, configured **both**, and left the choice to a decider, which was right. `audit-clean` survives because it is the interface other artifacts already name. **`audit-verdict-pass` is retired: no item carries it and no config names it. Do not use it.**

**THE ORDER OF THAT RETIREMENT IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING, because it generalises to every tag this detector reads.** The clean-verdict half is a string match between a tag on an item and a list in a config file, and the detector's failure mode is silence — so breaking the match in *either* direction turns an answered audit into an unreported one, and it surfaces as **one number moving in a line most people read as "green"**. So: confirm the surviving name is on the item, remove the retiring name from the item, prove by enumeration that no other item anywhere carries it, and only then delete it from the config. mg-9134 ran both halves of the break as experiments rather than arguing them (`code/audit_successor_consolidation_9134/`, arms N1 and N2): each drops `answered by a recorded clean verdict` from 1 to 0 and puts `mg-a0d6` back on the warn line. **Never delete a name from `clean_verdict_tags` while any item still carries it.**

**WHY THESE ARE FILING RULES AND NOT A CHECKLIST ITEM: they are what the audit-successor detector (pogo, mg-28b7) reads.** Armed narrow on `independent-audit` it examined **4 of this program's 9 merged audits and reported GREEN** — a 56% blind spot behind a green line, in an instrument whose entire failure mode is silence. It is now armed on `audit`, which is licensed by a measurement and not a preference: **all nine `done` items carrying `audit` are titled "INDEPENDENT AUDIT of ..."**, so it is not a loose marker here, while `independent-audit` is the tag people *forget* (five of the nine lack it). Arming on the forgettable tag made coverage depend on the habit that had already failed — **a convention does not fire when it applies, it fires when someone remembers it**, and that is the argument against fixing this by asking everyone to remember a second tag.

**THE ORDER WAS THE DECISION.** Widening first would have produced **2 false reports out of 3** on the store as it stood, and this detector refuses nothing and has no override, so a false report is pure noise — *and noise is how a check that blocks nothing gets ignored*. So: retro-link, then verdict-tag, then widen, then re-measure. Measured with only `[audit_successor]` varied: `["independent-audit"]` → 4 examined, 4 answered, 0 reported; `["audit"]` **before** the two fixes → 9 / 6 / 3, **two of them false**; `["audit"]` **after** → **9 examined, 9 answered, 0 reported, 0 false**. The instrument, its mutation arms and both transcripts are at `code/audit_successor_arming_a518/`; **C4 is the arm that matters** — with the four tags removed the detector names exactly the three audits it named before this landed, so the green is the fixes doing work rather than a second blind spot in a new place.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT BUY, and it is the same limit the detector states about itself: nothing checks that any audit was READ.** A successor ticket and a verdict tag are both artifacts anyone can produce cheaply — this rule produced four of them — and tagging an unread audit `audit-clean` silences the line exactly as filing an unread audit satisfies the pairing gate. **A false-report count of zero is not a statement that every audit was acted on.** The obligation to read the audit and mail what you find stays human and stays explicit, and it is not what any of this measures.

**AND A SECOND LIMIT, MEASURED BY mg-9134 AND SHARPER THAN THE FIRST: on 2026-08-12 nothing on this host ran the checklist this detector reports on.** The only caller of the audit-successor check in the whole of pogo is `pogo doctor --check` (`cmd/pogo/main.go`); pogod never invokes it. Nothing schedules that command — not launchd (15 `com.pogo.*` agents, none), not cron (no crontab), not `pogo schedule` (24 entries, none), not the deploy/watchdog/recovery/sleepwake scripts. Its one routine reader is the **doctor crew agent**, whose prompt makes it the first-line health check — and that agent was stopped on 2026-08-10 and has not run since. **So every measurement anyone has of this detector, mg-7ff8's and mg-a518's and mg-9134's alike, is a hand-run.** A hand-run proves the detector *can* speak; it does not prove the routine invocation reaches it with the same config and the same store, and here the routine invocation does not exist. **Arming a detector and observing it are two different acts, and only the first has been done for this one.**

**A THIRD, AND IT IS ABOUT WHAT A GREEN LINE CAN BE READ FOR.** The configured tag names are rendered by `cleanVerdictAdvice`, which is called from the **warn** branch of `auditSuccessorLine` and nowhere else. A **pass** prints counts and no tag list at all, so *no green run — hand-run or otherwise — can be read for which tag names were in force*. What a green run does discriminate is the `answered by a recorded clean verdict` count: `mg-a0d6` is hours past the window and carries only `audit-clean`, so a count of **1** there is reachable only if the list in force contained `audit-clean`. It cannot show that a retired name was dropped, because nothing carries the retired name — which is exactly the condition that made dropping it safe.

**Mixing ⊥ balance, now with a theorem (reference, 2026-07-19).** Leake–Lindberg–Oveis Gharan 2025 (arXiv:2503.01005, 𝒞-Lorentzian trickle-down) proves the flag complex of `J(P)` (whose maximal chains = linear extensions, by Birkhoff) is a **local spectral expander for every poset** ⟹ poly-time mixing of the down-up walk, arbitrary fields. Caveats: it is the *down-up* walk (not adjacent-transposition), exponent ~`n^{4–5}` (worse than Bubley–Dyer `n³ log n`, which stays best), and *poly* not entropy-rate — so the "ideal operator" is still open. Significance for us: if its expansion is uniform over all posets (apparent, unverified against the theorem statement), fast mixing holds even for near-counterexamples, so the `δ` obstruction is **provably not a mixing obstruction** — it lives in the *marginal*, exactly where mg-210d/dcae put it. Corrects an earlier pm-onethird claim that "spectral independence for `L(P)` is open." The one new object it hands us: the 𝒞-Lorentzian spectral-independence constant of `J(P)`, whose correlation (if any) with `δ` is an open, checkable question (expected: none, by the orthogonality above).

Empirically no poset reaches `δ ∈ (1/3, ≈0.354)`; best-known constructions plateau at `≈0.349` (Evan Chen; Sah; Olson–Sagan) — the forbidden-band signature, still open at `n → ∞`.
